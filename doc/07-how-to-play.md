# How to play Trailhead

Trailhead is an add-on for Minecraft Bedrock Edition (the version on phones, tablets, consoles, and
Windows). It adds giant sequoia groves to the Overworld: a new biome with trees up to 100 blocks tall,
their own wood, leaves, and saplings, and more to come. This page explains how to get it into a game,
and how to play it together on a Realm.

The add-on comes as one file, `Trailhead.mcaddon`. Ask Joel for the latest one.

## The one rule that matters

**Consoles (Switch, Xbox, PlayStation) cannot install an add-on file.** They can only *join* a world
that already has it: someone else's world, or a Realm. So one person in the group needs a phone,
tablet, or Windows PC to set the world up. After that, everyone plays on whatever they like.

Also: the groves only appear in land that is generated after the add-on is in place. Start a **new
world** for it. Adding it to an old world only gives groves in places nobody has explored yet.

## Installing on a phone, tablet, or Windows PC

1. Get `Trailhead.mcaddon` onto the device (email it to yourself, AirDrop it, put it in Google Drive,
   or copy it over a cable).
2. Open the file. On a phone or tablet, tap it in your downloads or files app and choose Minecraft if
   asked; on Windows, double-click it. Minecraft opens and says it is importing two packs.
3. Create a new world: **Play → Create New → Create New World**.
4. In the world settings on the left, choose **Behavior Packs**, open **My Packs**, find **Trailhead**,
   and press **Activate**. The matching resource pack switches on by itself (you may see a message
   saying so).
5. No **Experiments** need to be turned on. Leave them off.
6. Create the world.

You now have a Trailhead world. Saplings, logs, and the rest are in the creative inventory under
Nature and Construction, and groves generate where taiga forests would.

To update to a newer version of the add-on later: open the new `.mcaddon` the same way, then in the
world's Behavior Packs settings deactivate the old Trailhead and activate the new one.

## Playing together on a Realm (works for consoles)

A Realm is a world Minecraft keeps running for you on Mojang's servers, so friends can join any time
from any device. It needs a subscription, held by one person, the Realm's owner. Add-ons cannot be
added from inside the Realm menus; instead the owner prepares a world on a phone, tablet, or PC and
uploads it.

1. **Owner, on a phone, tablet, or Windows PC:** install the add-on and create a world as above. It is
   worth playing a minute in it to check it loads.
2. **Upload it to the Realm:** go to **Play → Realms tab**, press the pencil / edit button next to your
   Realm, and choose the world slot to replace. Look for **Replace World** (or **Upload World** /
   **Play on Realm** depending on the version), and pick the Trailhead world from your list. Confirm.
   The upload takes a few minutes. If the Realm already had a world people care about, replace a
   different slot, or back the old world up first (the Realm menu has **Download World**).
3. **Invite the others:** in the Realm's settings, **Members → Invite**, and enter their Xbox / Microsoft
   gamertags. They get the invitation in Minecraft under **Play → Realms** (and in the Friends tab).
4. **Everyone else, on any device including consoles:** accept the invitation, open Play → Realms, and
   join. The first time, the game downloads the Trailhead resource pack automatically (a few seconds).
   Nothing to install.

To update the add-on on a Realm, the owner repeats steps 1–2 with the newer `.mcaddon`, on the same
world: **Download World** from the Realm first, open it locally, swap the packs as described under
Installing, then **Replace World** with it. Progress made on the Realm since the download would be
lost, so do it when nobody is mid-project, or download right before uploading.

## Playing together without a Realm

Free, but the host has to be online for anyone to play. Someone with a phone, tablet, or PC installs
the add-on and creates the world with **Multiplayer** on (it is on by default). Friends on the same
Microsoft account friends list see the world under **Play → Friends** while it is open, consoles
included, and join from there. Players in the same house can also join over the home Wi-Fi.

## What to look for in the game

- Sequoia groves are found where taiga forests would otherwise be: cool, rainy country with spruces.
  Look for very tall trunks above the treeline.
- The forest has an age to it: some parts are thickets of young trees, others are ancient with
  scattered giants and podzol underfoot. The biggest trees are the rarest.
- Sequoia logs craft into planks like any wood, an axe strips them, and leaves occasionally drop a
  sapling. A planted sapling grows into a small sequoia.

## If something goes wrong

- **The packs did not import.** Make sure the file ends in `.mcaddon` (some phones rename it to `.zip`;
  rename it back). On iOS, use the Files app and choose "Share → Minecraft" if tapping does nothing.
- **Trees look like purple-and-black checkers, or there are no groves.** The resource pack is not
  active or is out of date. In the world settings check that Trailhead is active under both Behavior
  Packs and Resource Packs; on a Realm, leave and rejoin so the pack downloads again.
- **A console player cannot join a Realm.** Their game must be on the same version as the Realm; the
  Switch in particular sometimes gets updates a few days late. Check for a game update on the console.
- **Something else.** Tell Joel what you saw, and what device and Minecraft version you are on.
